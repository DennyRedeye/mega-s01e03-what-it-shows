Shared parser: month-first interpretation

The shared parser reads dates month first, but the tenant directory identifies day-first locales. The exported desk classifier counts app and email reports, and booking-v2 form reports, under parser. It also counts the timing cases there, with a footnote; the count is not proof that the parser explains those bodies. Browser locale and intake tags are not substitutes for the tenant’s configuration.

R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July. R-41205 comes from Morrow Tax, whose tenant locale is fr-FR, even though intake saw an en-US browser and tagged it as a hint problem. The engineering update reports that the parser now parses dd/mm for EU locales, so the parser ticket is completed. The export explains the original failure; it does not prove the implementation details, regression coverage or rollout of the reported fix.

booking-v3: the form tells people the wrong date order

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour use that form with day-first tenant locales. People follow the month-first hint, but the form reads their input in the tenant’s day-first order. This is a separate interface contract problem, not the shared parser fix.

R-41230 is a Copper Studio form report: 6 January becomes 1 June. Engineering retired booking-v3 and marked the hint as won’t fix, so the hint ticket is closed as not planned, not completed by a date-hint patch. The historical reports remain in the audit.

Timing: keep the uncertainty visible

The desk notes identify reports with adjacent-day or other timing symptoms. The tenant-only classifier files them under parser, but a month/day swap does not explain their reported behavior. R-41236 says an invite arrived before its booking; that observation is not enough to prove a one-day shift.

The investigation must retain the original booking date, timezone, serialized timestamp, recipient timezone and delivery logs. Test a single event across Lisbon and Toronto and check date-only values around midnight and daylight-saving boundaries. These are diagnostic checks, not a claim that a timezone bug has already been reproduced.

Why the merge hid separate problems

Decision 0001 permits merges on normalized subject alone, without reading the body. The accepted triage page still repeats that rule, even though its interactive example computes cause using tenant locale and channel. Neither subject equality nor that helper alone proves that the bodies describe the same failure.

Match tenant configuration, input path, observed date transformation and reproduction evidence before merging. Keep unresolved timing reports separate. Do not rewrite the accepted decision: propose a new decision that replaces subject-only merging after desk approval.
